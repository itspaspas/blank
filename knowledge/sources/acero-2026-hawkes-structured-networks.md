## Kernthesen

### 1. ⭐ Realistic order flow makes market making infinite-dimensional and non-Markovian (pp. 1–2)

The authors start from the empirical stylised facts: high-frequency order flow shows
"pronounced clustering, self-excitation, and cross-excitation across order types and
assets" (p. 1), and Hawkes processes are the parsimonious model for this. The decisive step
is the kernel shape: empirical studies indicate kernels "often exhibit slow, power-law decay
rather than simple exponential decay [19, 25, 27]. This renders the associated market-making
problem intrinsically infinite-dimensional and non-Markovian, as the relevant state depends on
the entire history of order events." (p. 1). Footnote 1 (p. 2) cites the
Hardiman–Bercot–Bouchaud estimate $\alpha\approx0.15$ for S&P futures, the value later used for
the hardest experiment (ε = 0.15).

### 2. Classical HJB market making rests on restrictive assumptions (p. 1)

The stochastic-control literature (AS 2008, GLFT 2013, BEGV 2021, CJR 2014, Cartea et al.
2015 and others, refs. [3, 6, 7, 9, 14, 15, 22]) derives quotes "by solving the associated
Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equations" but typically relies on "memoryless Poisson
arrivals, low-dimensional Markovian representations of order-flow dynamics, and fully
specified arrival processes and fill probabilities" (p. 1). The paper positions itself against
all three assumptions at once; it does not engage with any specific closed form.

### 3. ⭐ The two-stage "estimate-then-control" paradigm is fragile (pp. 1–2)

Existing non-exponential Hawkes market making is model-based: `lai-2023-data-driven-deep`
estimates a power-law kernel and then solves by deep learning; [[martin-2026-market-making-hawkes]]
"constructs a high-dimensional Markovian approximation of a given kernel and solves the
corresponding HJB equations using deep learning" (p. 1). This system-identification-then-control
pipeline "is sensitive to kernel misspecification", and under regime changes in the kernel or
fill function "such a model-based approach cannot adapt effectively and may suffer significant
performance degradation over time" (p. 1). The claim is argued, not demonstrated: no
model-based baseline appears in the experiments (see Kernthesis 21).

### 4. Prior RL for market making is Markovian; model-free non-Markovian Hawkes work is nearly absent (p. 2)

Most RL market-making papers assume Poisson flow (`cao-2026-logarithmic-regret-ergodic`,
`gueant-2019-deep-reinforcement-learning`, and others) or exponential Hawkes
(`gasperov-2022-deep-reinforcement-learning`). The only other model-free RL work with
non-Markovian Hawkes dynamics is `jain-2025-impulse-control-hawkes`, "where the agent learns when
to intervene rather than continuously quoting bid and ask spreads, and the policy neither
exploits Hawkes structure nor provides performance guarantees" (p. 2).

### 5. The truncation literature treats uncontrolled dynamics; this paper carries the error into control (p. 2)

Hawkes and Volterra processes with general kernels "admit infinite-dimensional Markovian lifts"
(refs. [10, 18]); truncation error is analysed by Alfonsi–Kebaier 2024 and Bayer–Breneis 2023
(refs. [2, 8]). "However, these works focus primarily on uncontrolled dynamics. We extend this
line of research to sequential decision-making problems by quantifying how the approximation
error propagates to control performance" (p. 2).

### 6. ⭐ Model: Avellaneda–Stoikov with two independent Hawkes market-order streams (p. 2)

Single asset; $M^+$ and $M^-$ are "two independent Hawkes processes" (p. 2), with intensity
$\lambda^c_t=\lambda^c_0(t)+\int_{[0,t)}g^c(t-s)\,dM^c_s$, $c\in\{+,-\}$ — so **self-excitation
only, no cross-side excitation** in the theory. Mid-price $S_t=S_0+\sigma W_t+\epsilon^+M^+_t-\epsilon^-M^-_t$
(each market order has permanent impact). The maker posts predictable spreads
$(\delta^-_t,\delta^+_t)\in[\underline\delta,\bar\delta]^2$; an arrival on side $c$ is executed with
probability $p^c_t=p^c(\delta^c_t)$. Arrivals are exogenous: **all market orders** excite the flow,
the maker's quotes only thin them into fills. Policies are event-driven: quotes are functions of
the event history $((\tau_k,c_k)_{k\le j},Q_{\tau_j})$, piecewise constant between arrivals (p. 2).

### 7. ⭐ Objective (2) is risk-adjusted expected wealth, not CARA (p. 2)

$J^g(\pi)=\mathbb E[R^g_\pi]$, $R^g_\pi:=X_T+Q_T(S_T-\kappa Q_T)-\gamma\int_0^TQ_t^2\,dt$ — eq. (2),
the Cartea–Jaimungal–Ricci form with terminal liquidation penalty $\kappa$ and running penalty
$\gamma$. "Since the order-flow processes $M^\pm$ are non-Markovian for general excitation
kernels, solving (2) is computationally challenging even under complete knowledge of $g^\pm$ and
$p^\pm$." (p. 2). The paper then assumes neither is known.

### 8. Kernels are assumed completely monotone via a Bernstein representation (p. 2)

Assumption (1): $g^c(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta t}\,\vartheta^c(d\beta)$ with $g^c\in L^2([0,T])$
and $\|g^c\|_{L^1([0,T])}<1$ (subcritical **on the horizon**, not on $[0,\infty)$). Example 1:
mixture-of-exponentials $g=\sum_{j=1}^{p_0}\alpha_je^{-\beta_jt}$ has $\vartheta=\sum\alpha_j\delta_{\beta_j}$;
shifted power law $g(t)=\gamma(t+\tau_0)^{-(1+\alpha)}$ has
$\vartheta(d\beta)=\gamma\beta^\alpha e^{-\beta\tau_0}d\beta/\Gamma(1+\alpha)$ (p. 2). Note the
symbol $\gamma$ is reused here as the kernel scale.

### 9. ⭐ Kernel truncation by quadrature of the Bernstein integral, eq. (3) (p. 3)

On $[K_1,K_2)$ split into $p$ equal cells $I_j^{K_1,K_2,p}$, take
$\hat g^{K_1,K_2,p}(t):=\sum_{j=1}^p\vartheta(I_j^{K_1,K_2,p})\,e^{-\beta_j^{K_1,K_2,p}t}$ with
$\beta_j\in I_j$ — eq. (3). Under this mixture-of-$p$-exponentials kernel "the market-making problem
admits a finite-dimensional Markovian formulation" (p. 3). Mass of $\vartheta$ below $K_1$ and above
$K_2$ is simply dropped.

### 10. ⭐ Proposition 1: $L^2$ kernel error = lower tail + upper tail + $O(1/p)$ quadrature (p. 3)

$\|g-\hat g^{K_1,K_2,p}\|_{L^2([0,T])}\le\varepsilon_l(K_1)+\varepsilon_u(K_2)+\dfrac{T^{3/2}(K_2-K_1)\,\vartheta([K_1,K_2))}{\sqrt3\,p}$,
with $\varepsilon_l(K_1)=\|\int_0^{K_1}e^{-\beta\cdot}\vartheta(d\beta)\|_{L^2}$,
$\varepsilon_u(K_2)=\|\int_{K_2}^\infty e^{-\beta\cdot}\vartheta(d\beta)\|_{L^2}$. The proof uses the
pointwise quadrature bound $|\hat g-g^{K_1,K_2}|\le p^{-1}t(K_2-K_1)\vartheta([K_1,K_2))$ following
Alfonsi–Kebaier [2, Cor. 3.1]. The approximation is exact for a finite mixture once the support is
inside $[K_1,K_2)$; for the power law the tail rates are said to follow by "direct computation" but
are **not stated** (p. 3).

### 11. ⭐⭐ Theorem 1: regret of the truncated-kernel optimal policy is $O(\sqrt{\text{kernel error}})$ (p. 3)

Let $\hat\pi\in\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}J^{\hat g}(\pi)$ be optimal for the truncated problem, and
$\mathrm{Reg}(\hat\pi):=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}J^g(\pi)-J^g(\hat\pi)$ (true kernel). If there exists
$\rho^*\in[0,1)$ with $\|\hat g^{\pm,K_1,K_2,p}\|_{L^1([0,T])}\le\rho^*$, then
$$\mathrm{Reg}(\hat\pi)\le C\sum_{c\in\{+,-\}}\Big(\varepsilon^c_l(K_1)+\varepsilon^c_u(K_2)+\frac{T^{3/2}(K_2-K_1)\vartheta^c([K_1,K_2))}{\sqrt3\,p}\Big)^{1/2},$$
"where $C>0$ depends on model parameters and $\rho^*$, but is independent of $K_1,K_2$ and $p$"
(p. 3). The stability condition holds whenever $\vartheta^\pm(\{0\})=0$, $K_1$ is small and $K_2,p$
are large. The rate in $p$ is therefore $p^{-1/2}$, and the bound is a square root of the $L^2$
kernel error.

### 12. ⭐ Lemma 1: value perturbation via a Poisson-random-measure coupling (p. 7)

$\sup_{\pi\in\Pi}|J^g(\pi)-J^{\hat g}(\pi)|\le\hat C\big(\sum_c\hat H^c\|g^c-\hat g^c\|_{L^2([0,T])}\big)^{1/2}$,
$\hat H^c=\dfrac{\Lambda_cT}{(1-\|g^c\|_{L^1})(1-\|\hat g^c\|_{L^1})}$, $\hat C=2\sqrt2\,\|\hat R^*\|_{L^2}T^{1/4}$,
with the dominating reward $\hat R^*:=|x_0|+(S^*+\bar\delta)C_{M,\hat M}+Q^*S^*+(\kappa+\gamma T)(Q^*)^2$ — eq. (6).
True and approximate Hawkes processes, and their executions, are built by thinning the **same**
Poisson random measures (construction "as in [32, Appendix A]", i.e. [[martin-2026-market-making-hawkes]]);
the rewards coincide outside the event $A$ that an arrival or execution mismatch occurs before $T$,
so by Cauchy–Schwarz $|J^g(\pi)-J^{\hat g}(\pi)|\le2\|\hat R^*\|_{L^2}(\mathbb P[A])^{1/2}$ — eq. (7) — and
$\mathbb P[A]$ is bounded by expected mismatch counts (p. 7). The square root in Theorem 1 comes from
this Cauchy–Schwarz step, uniformly over all policies; no property of the value function is used.

### 13. Proof of Theorem 1 closes the argument by uniform moment bounds (p. 7)

Because $\|\hat g\|_{L^1}\le\rho^*<1$, Hawkes moments are bounded uniformly in $\rho^*$ (Leblanc [31]),
so $\|\hat R^*\|_{L^2}<\infty$ via Doob's maximal inequality; then
$\sup_\pi J^g(\pi)-J^g(\hat\pi)\le2\sup_{\pi}|J^g(\pi)-J^{\hat g}(\pi)|$, and Proposition 1 plus Lemma 1
give the result (p. 7).

### 14. ⭐ The truncated problem is Markovian in exponentially weighted features, eq. (4) (p. 3)

With $h^c_j(t)=\alpha^c_j\sum_{i:\tau_i\le t,c_i=c}e^{-\beta^c_j(t-\tau_i)}$, $j=1,\dots,p$ — eq. (4) —
the truncated problem "is Markovian in the augmented state $(\hat h_j)_{j=1,\dots,p}$ … so its
optimal policy takes the processes (4) as feedback variables. By Theorem 1, this policy is nearly
optimal for the true problem once $p$ is sufficiently large." (p. 3). The paper never writes, solves
or characterises the HJB equation of this lifted problem; the statement is used only to
motivate the policy class.

### 15. Only the most recent $L$ events matter in practice (p. 3)

Because $\beta^c_j\ge K_1>0$, the contribution of a past arrival to $h^c_j$ decays exponentially, so
the sum in (4) "can be restricted in practice to the most recent $L$ events without material loss of
information" (p. 3). The experiments use $L=32$ (p. 4).

### 16. ⭐ The Hawkes-Structured Network (HSN): a learnable $C\times p$ mixture-of-exponentials filter (pp. 3–4)

For a $C$-channel process ($C=2m$ for $m$ assets: bid and ask per asset), HSN computes
$h^{c,l}_j(t)=\alpha^{c,l}_j\sum_{i:\tau^l_i\le t,c_i=c}\exp(-\beta^{c,l}_j(t-\tau^l_i))$ — eq. (5) —
with learnable $(\alpha,\beta)\in(0,\infty)^2$, over the last $L$ events. The $C\times p$ features are
concatenated with $(S_t,q_t,T-t)$ and fed into an MLP; outputs are squashed by a sigmoid into the
spread range, giving a mean $\mu_\theta(x)$ of a Gaussian policy
$\pi_\theta(d\delta\mid x)=\mathcal N(\delta;\mu_\theta(x),e^{2\sigma_a}I_C)\,d\delta$ (p. 4). Policy and
value networks share the feature extractor (p. 3). Each channel is filtered **only by its own
events**: there is no cross-channel term in (5).

### 17. ⭐ End-to-end PPO training learns a task-oriented Markov approximation (p. 4)

The filter parameters are trained jointly with all other parameters by PPO to maximise (2); "HSN
learns a task-oriented Markovian approximation directly from interactions with the underlying
non-Markovian Hawkes process". This "bypasses the model-estimation-then-optimization procedure"
of classical multi-stage pipelines (CJR [15], Martin–Tse [32]) (p. 4).

### 18. Efficiency: $Cp$ features instead of $CL$ (p. 4)

The filter compresses $L$ events into a $Cp$-dimensional vector "with $p\ll L$", versus $CL$
dimensions for frame stacking or an equivalent recurrent hidden state; the MLP "receives a
structured low-dimensional representation rather than having to infer the underlying Hawkes state
directly from the raw event stream", which "reduces the effective search space for RL algorithms and
improves sample efficiency" (p. 4).

### 19. Domain-informed initialisation and positivity (p. 4)

Decays $\beta$ can be initialised to span relevant time scales (fast and slow for $p=2$); amplitudes
"can be initialized with small positive values such that the initial branching ratio $\sum_j\alpha_j/\beta_j$
remains well below the subcriticality threshold"; positivity via softplus reparametrisation (p. 4).

### 20. ⭐ The theory–practice link is qualitative: HSN approximates greedy policies "up to RL training error" (pp. 3–4)

"Moreover, the regret analysis in Theorem 1 applies to policies learned on top of these filtered
features up to RL training error." (p. 3). For large $p$ and an expressive MLP, "the HSN policy class
approximates arbitrarily well the greedy policies induced by the mixture-of-$p$-exponential
approximation (3)", so model-free RL "can achieve performance at least comparable to these greedy
policies" (p. 4). No bound on the RL training error, the MLP approximation error, or the joint learning
of $\beta$ is given; Theorem 1 covers the exact optimiser $\hat\pi$ with **fixed** quadrature nodes.

### 21. Experimental protocol: CJR-style environment, PPO, six neural baselines only (pp. 4–5)

$\sigma=0.1$, $S_0=100$, $\epsilon^\pm=0$ ("a pure diffusion midprice, so that executed trades do not
directly move the midprice"), $q_0=0$, $\gamma=0.10$, $\kappa=0.001$, $|Q|\le5$, $L=32$; $T=0.5$
(exponential, multi-asset, fractional) or $T=1.0$ (regime switching); spreads
$(\delta^+,\delta^-)\in[0.005,0.30]^2$; fill $p^\pm(\delta)=\exp(-10\delta)$ "as in [15]". PPO (clip 0.2,
GAE 0.95, discount 0.99, entropy 0.003, value coef. 0.5, Adam $3\times10^{-4}$, 100 updates of 1024
rollout steps, minibatch 256, grad-clip 0.5). Evaluation: greedy policy over 100 fixed-seed episodes,
mean ± SEM over 30 seeds, Welch $t$-tests (p. 4). HSN uses $p=2$ modes per channel. Baselines
(≈2.3–2.8k parameters each, parameter-matched): MLP+frame-stack, MLP+EMA (8 fixed decays
$\beta_{\rm EMA}\in\{0.1,\dots,8.0\}$), GRU, LSTM, Transformer, and a no-history Simple MLP (p. 5). **No
non-neural baseline** — no AS/GLFT/CJR closed form, no constant quote, no Poisson-model quotes, no
numerical HJB optimum, no model-based (estimate-then-control) policy.

### 22. ⭐ Four environment families, seven configurations (p. 5)

(1) single-asset two-mode mixture of exponentials, $(\beta_1,\beta_2)=(5.0,0.4)$, $\alpha_j=(n/2)\beta_j$,
$n\in\{0.75,0.90\}$, $\lambda_0^+=1.0$, $\lambda_0^-=0.7$ — a **control**, because "the leading-order
optimal spread of [3, 15] depends only weakly on the intensity in this regime"; (2) two-asset,
four-channel cross-excited Hawkes (see Kernthesis 23); (3) single-asset shifted power law
$g(t)=c(t+\tau_0)^{-(1+\varepsilon)}$, $\tau_0=1$, $c=n\varepsilon\tau_0^\varepsilon$, $n=0.7$,
$\varepsilon\in\{0.50,0.15\}$ — it "admits no exact finite mixture-of-exponentials representation,
stressing the kernel-approximation axis of Theorem 1"; (4) regime switching between branching
ratios $(0.50,0.85)$ by an unobserved continuous-time Markov chain with switch rate $sr\in\{2,4\}$ (p. 5).

### 23. ⭐⭐ The multi-asset environment: same-side cross-asset excitation, ρ = 0.40 (p. 5)

"four-channel Hawkes $M=(M^{A,+},M^{A,-},M^{B,+},M^{B,-})$ on two assets $\{A,B\}$, with kernel matrix
$g_{ij}(t)=\alpha_{ij}(\tfrac12e^{-5.0t}+\tfrac12e^{-0.3t})$; with off-diagonal entries coupling the same
side across assets ($A^+\leftrightarrow B^+$ and $A^-\leftrightarrow B^-$), so the per-channel self- and
cross-excitation branching are $n(1-cs)/2=0.12$ and $n\cdot cs/2=0.28$, giving a spectral radius
$\rho(M)=n/2=0.40$ (with input branching budget $n=0.80$)" (p. 5), $cs=0.7$. Baselines
$\lambda_0^{A,\pm}=1.5$, $\lambda_0^{B,\pm}=1.2$; $S_0^A=100$, $S_0^B=50$. "The agent posts four
continuous spreads, the reward aggregates cash plus inventory penalties across assets." (p. 5). Not
specified: per-asset $\sigma$, correlation of the two Brownian motions, per-asset inventory caps, whether
the fill function differs by asset, and whether the penalty includes cross-inventory terms (presumably
the sum of two eq. (2) penalties — inferred).

### 24. ⭐ HSN wins on the five intensity-sensitive configurations by 5–33 % and ties on the exponential controls (pp. 5–6, Table 1)

"HSN attains the highest mean cumulative reward on the five intensity-sensitive configurations
(columns 3–7 of Table 1), with $p<0.01$ to $p<0.001$ across 30 seeds each. The largest advantages occur
in the heavy-tailed fractional regime (+32.5% at ε = 0.15), the multi-asset cross-excitation setting
(+16.6% at cs = 0.7), and the regime-switching setting (+11.1% at sr = 2)." (p. 5). Simple MLP trails HSN
by 36–57 % on these columns, attributing the gain to use of event history. On the exponential controls
HSN is statistically tied with MLP+frame-stack (0.0171 vs 0.0184 at $n=0.75$; 0.0208 vs 0.0211 at

#### (1) The lift as code: Bernstein measure and the quadrature of eq. (3)

**Bernstein representation**, eq. (1) (p. 2):
$$g^c(t)=\int_0^\infty e^{-\beta t}\,\vartheta^c(d\beta),\qquad t>0,$$
$\vartheta^c$ a measure on $[0,\infty)$, $g^c\in L^2([0,T])$, $\|g^c\|_{L^1([0,T])}<1$. Example 1 (p. 2)
gives, for the shifted power law $g(t)=\gamma(t+\tau_0)^{-(1+\alpha)}$, the density
$\vartheta(d\beta)=\gamma\beta^\alpha e^{-\beta\tau_0}\,d\beta/\Gamma(1+\alpha)$ (source symbols; $\gamma\equiv c$,
$\alpha\equiv\varepsilon$ of § 5).

**Truncation, eq. (3)** (p. 3), verbatim in content: for $0\le K_1<K_2<\infty$, $p\in\mathbb N$,
$$\hat g^{K_1,K_2,p}(t):=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\vartheta\big(I_j^{K_1,K_2,p}\big)\,e^{-\beta_j^{K_1,K_2,p}t},\qquad
I_j^{K_1,K_2,p}=\Big[K_1+\tfrac{(j-1)(K_2-K_1)}{p},\;K_1+\tfrac{j(K_2-K_1)}{p}\Big),\qquad \beta_j^{K_1,K_2,p}\in I_j^{K_1,K_2,p}.$$

What exactly is chosen:

- **Variable:** the decay rate $\beta$ itself, on a **linear** scale. **Not log-spaced.**
- **Cells:** $p$ **equal-width** cells of width $h=(K_2-K_1)/p$ on $[K_1,K_2)$.
- **Weights:** $w_j=\vartheta(I_j)$, the **exact Bernstein mass of the cell**. They are not
  density × quadrature weight.
- **Nodes:** any $\beta_j\in I_j$. The source leaves the choice open (left end, midpoint, …). The
  bound holds for every choice, because $|e^{-\beta t}-e^{-\beta_jt}|\le t\,h$ on the cell.
- **Type:** a composite rectangle ("histogram") rule following Alfonsi–Kebaier [2, Cor. 3.1].
  It is **not Gauss–Legendre and not Gauss–Jacobi**. No Gauss-type rule appears anywhere in the
  paper.
- **Dropped mass:** everything below $K_1$ and above $K_2$ is dropped, through the tail kernels
  $g_l^{K_1}(t):=\int_0^{K_1}e^{-\beta t}\vartheta(d\beta)$ and $g_u^{K_2}(t):=\int_{K_2}^\infty e^{-\beta t}\vartheta(d\beta)$
  (p. 3). Their norms are $\varepsilon_l(K_1):=\|g_l^{K_1}\|_{L^2([0,T])}$ and
  $\varepsilon_u(K_2):=\|g_u^{K_2}\|_{L^2([0,T])}$ (p. 3).
- **No values given.** The paper gives no numerical $K_1$, $K_2$, node rule or $p$ for eq. (3), and
  never computes the quadrature lift. Its experiments *learn* $\beta$ instead (HSN). The only
  log-spacing in the paper is the Fig. 2 **initialisation** of the learned rates: "log-spaced over
  $[0.1,10]$ ($\beta=1$ at $p=1$) with $\alpha_j=(n/p)\beta_j$" (p. 6).
- **The $K_1=0$ trap:** $K_1=0$ is admitted ($0\le K_1$). The practical "last $L$ events"
  truncation, however, needs $\beta_j\ge K_1>0$ (p. 3).

*Closed forms for coding the shifted power law, $\vartheta(d\beta)=c\,\beta^{\varepsilon}e^{-\beta\tau_0}d\beta/\Gamma(1+\varepsilon)$.
These are this wiki's derivation, not printed in the source; checked against numerical
quadrature of eq. (1) on 2026-11-01:*
$$\vartheta([a,b))=c\,\tau_0^{-(1+\varepsilon)}\big[P(1+\varepsilon,\tau_0 b)-P(1+\varepsilon,\tau_0 a)\big],\qquad \vartheta([0,\infty))=g(0)=c\,\tau_0^{-(1+\varepsilon)},$$
$$g_l^{K_1}(t)=c\,(t+\tau_0)^{-(1+\varepsilon)}P\big(1+\varepsilon,K_1(t+\tau_0)\big),\qquad g_u^{K_2}(t)=c\,(t+\tau_0)^{-(1+\varepsilon)}Q\big(1+\varepsilon,K_2(t+\tau_0)\big),$$
with $P$/$Q$ the regularised lower/upper incomplete gamma functions (`scipy.special.gammainc`/`gammaincc`).

```python
edges = K1 + (K2 - K1) * np.arange(p + 1) / p                    # eq. (3) cells
w     = c * tau0**(-(1 + eps)) * (gammainc(1 + eps, tau0 * edges[1:])
                                  - gammainc(1 + eps, tau0 * edges[:-1]))   # theta(I_j)
beta  = 0.5 * (edges[:-1] + edges[1:])   # node: midpoint (source leaves the choice open)
```

    small, and $K_2,p$ are sufficiently large" (p. 3).
- **$C$ is not specified.** It can be traced through § 6 (p. 7):
  $\mathrm{Reg}\le2\sup_\pi|J^g-J^{\hat g}|\le2\hat C\big(\sum_c\hat H^c\|g^c-\hat g^c\|_{L^2}\big)^{1/2}$, with
  $\hat C=2\sqrt2\,\|\hat R^*\|_{L^2}T^{1/4}$ and
  $\hat H^c=\Lambda_cT/\big((1-\|g^c\|_{L^1[0,T]})(1-\|\hat g^c\|_{L^1[0,T]})\big)$.
  - *Inferred (this wiki):* $C=4\sqrt2\,T^{1/4}\|\hat R^*\|_{L^2}\max_c(\hat H^c)^{1/2}$.
  - $\|\hat R^*\|_{L^2}$ (eq. (6)) needs second moments of $|x_0|+(S^*+\bar\delta)C_{M,\hat M}+Q^*S^*+(\kappa+\gamma T)(Q^*)^2$.
    That means fourth moments of the Hawkes counts, which are bounded only abstractly (Leblanc
    [31], Doob) and **never evaluated**.
- **Nature of the bound:**
  - It is a worst-case **upper** bound. The same estimate bounds the value error
    $\sup_\pi|J^g(\pi)-J^{\hat g}(\pi)|$, and the only use of $\hat\pi$'s optimality is
    $\mathrm{Reg}\le2\sup_\pi|\Delta J|$ (p. 7).
  - There is no lower bound and no second-order (quadratic-in-error) expansion of regret.
  - The square root comes from Cauchy–Schwarz on the coupling-mismatch event, eq. (7) (p. 7).

#### (4) The experimental power-law kernels → project notation

- **Table 1, setting (3)** (p. 5): "Single-asset, shifted power-law kernel (ε ∈ {0.50, 0.15}): the
  two-channel setup of (1) with kernel $g(t)=c(t+\tau_0)^{-(1+\varepsilon)}$, $\tau_0=1$,
  $c=n\varepsilon\tau_0^\varepsilon$, so $\|g\|_{L^1([0,\infty))}=n$, $n=0.7$, and ε = 0.15 matching
  the empirical Hardiman–Bercot–Bouchaud regime [25]."
  - Shared settings: $T=0.5$, $\lambda_0^+=1.0$, $\lambda_0^-=0.7$, fill $e^{-10\delta}$, $\gamma=0.10$,
    $\kappa=0.001$, $|Q|\le5$, $\sigma=0.1$, $\epsilon^\pm=0$, $L=32$, spreads in $[0.005,0.30]$
    (pp. 4–5).
- **Fig. 2, kernel frac015** (p. 6): "a shifted power-law with ε = 0.15, $\tau_0$ = 0.1 (frac015). The
  horizon T = 10 lets the long-memory intensity build up, and $\tau_0$ = 0.1 makes the power-law
  kernel less regular near t = 0", at $n=0.85$.
  - $c$ is not restated. Presumably it is again $n\varepsilon\tau_0^\varepsilon$ (inferred).
- **Mapping to project notation (exact):**
  $c(t+\tau_0)^{-(1+\varepsilon)}$ with $c=n\varepsilon\tau_0^\varepsilon$ ≡ $\rho(b-1)t_0^{b-1}(t+t_0)^{-b}$,
  where $b=1+\varepsilon$, $\rho=n$, $t_0=\tau_0$, and the tail index is $\alpha_A=b-1=\varepsilon$.
  - The experiments therefore use $b\in\{1.15,1.5\}$, $\rho=0.7$, $t_0=1=2T$ (Table 1) and
    $b=1.15$, $\rho=0.85$, $t_0=0.1=0.01\,T$ (Fig. 2).
  - The Fig. 2 ratio $t_0/T=0.01$ equals the plan's fixed $t_0=0.01T$.
  - The source never writes "1.15": its $\beta$ always denotes a decay rate.
- *Horizon mass, inferred:* $\|g\|_{L^1[0,T]}=n\big(1-(\tau_0/(T+\tau_0))^{\varepsilon}\big)$.
  - Table 1: 0.041 for ε = 0.15 and 0.128 for ε = 0.50, i.e. **5.9 % / 18.4 % of $\rho$ inside the
    episode**. The long tail sits almost entirely beyond $T$, and Theorem 1's subcriticality
    condition is trivially met.
  - Fig. 2: 0.425, i.e. 50 % of $\rho$.

#### (6) Table 1 and the "tie" regime (pp. 5–6)

> "On the two single-asset exponential controls, HSN is statistically tied with the strongest
> baseline, MLP+frame-stack (0.0171 vs 0.0184 at $n$ = 0.75 and 0.0208 vs 0.0211 at $n$ = 0.90, both
> $p$ > 0.05), while outperforming every other architecture (including the no-memory Simple MLP) by
> 45–90%. This is consistent with the near-intensity-independence of the optimal spread in that
> regime: the intensity representation carries little policy-value signal, so HSN matches the best
> general-purpose baseline rather than exceeding it." (p. 5)

- **Prediction stated in advance:** setting (1) already announces the tie: "The leading-order
  optimal spread of [3, 15] depends only weakly on the intensity in this regime, so the theory
  predicts little intensity-representation advantage; we retain it as a control." (p. 5)
- **What the paper does not do:**
  - No formula for this "leading-order optimal spread" is written, and no closed-form quote is run.
  - The explanation is a qualitative appeal to AS/CJR.
  - In both tie columns, HSN's mean is *below* MLP+frame-stack's.
- **Internal inconsistency** (Table 1, checked on the page image): at $n=0.90$, HSN beats MLP+EMA by
  only +17.5 % (0.0208 vs 0.0177) and Simple MLP by +44 % (0.0208 vs 0.0144). Both fall outside
  the stated "45–90%".
- **Reward at $p=2$, fractional columns** (HSN / Simple MLP / MLP+EMA / MLP+frame-stack):
  - ε = 0.50: .0217 / .0125 / .0163 / .0206 (HSN +5.3 %**);
  - ε = 0.15: .0216 / .0093 / .0127 / .0163 (HSN +32.5 %***).
  - Simple MLP has SEM ±.0000 in every single-asset column, i.e. a seed-independent
    (near-constant) policy.
- ⚠ *Reproduction flag (this wiki's Monte-Carlo, 8,000 paths per cell, 2026-11-01). The setting
  read as printed: empty history at $t=0$, $\lambda_0=(1.0,0.7)$, $T=0.5$, $\gamma=0.1$,
  $\kappa=0.001$, $k=10$.*
  - An episode then carries ≈ 0.9 (power law) to ≈ 1.2 (exponential) market orders in total.

#### (7) Which p the experiments use; which functional of the history the quote depends on

- **$p$ in the experiments:**
  - Main experiments: "HSN uses $p$ = 2 modes per channel." (p. 4)
  - Table 2 uses $p=3$; Fig. 2 uses $p\in\{1,\dots,8\}$.
- **Initialisations:**
  - Exponential configuration: $(\beta_1,\beta_2)=(6.0,0.4)$, $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)=(1.5,0.1)$, "an
    initial branching ratio of 0.5 with neither decay rate coinciding with a true kernel mode"
    (p. 4).
  - "The remaining configurations initialize at $(\beta_1,\beta_2)=(5.0,0.3)$ and
    $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)=(0.2,0.4)$" (pp. 4–5).
  - *Inferred:* the second initialisation coincides with the true decays $(5.0,0.3)$ of the
    multi-asset kernel (and, via "the setup of (1)", of the regime-switching kernel).
  - *Inferred:* it gives $\sum_j\alpha_j/\beta_j=1.37>1$, against p. 4's advice to stay "well below
    the subcriticality threshold".
- **The functional of the history:**
  - *True problem:* policies are arbitrary measurable maps of the whole event history,
    $\delta_t=\Phi^c_j((\tau_k,c_k)_{k\le j},Q_{\tau_j})$ (p. 2).
  - *Truncated problem:* it "is Markovian in the augmented state $(\hat h^c_j)_{j=1,\dots,p}$ with
    $\hat\alpha^c_j=\vartheta^c(I_j^{K_1,K_2,p})$ and $\hat\beta^c_j=\beta_j^{c,K_1,K_2,p}$, so its optimal
    policy takes the processes (4) as feedback variables" (p. 3).
  - The feedback is therefore the **vector** of $p$ exponentially weighted counts per side, plus
    $(t,Q)$ (and $S$ in HSN). It is not a scalar intensity.
  - The paper never characterises the optimal feedback map. It never states that the quote depends
    only on $\lambda_t$ or on a forecast curve.
  - *Inferred (this wiki):* the vector $(\hat h_j)$ is in one-to-one linear correspondence with the
    $p$-factor forecast from past events, $\hat m_t(u)-\lambda_0=\sum_j\hat h_j(t)e^{-\hat\beta_ju}$. The
    "current intensity" $\hat\lambda=\lambda_0+\sum_jh_j$ (p. 6) is its value at $u=0$. "Depends on
    $h$" is thus the project's "depends on the $p$-factor forecast curve".

### Bearing on the memory-regret gap (plan Task 2 stop rule)

**Does this source already report regret/reward vs $p$ on a power-law kernel with an exact or
near-exact reference AND a second-order regret law? — No.**

1. **No reward or regret vs $p$.** Reward exists only at $p=2$ (Table 1, p. 6). The $p$-sweep
   (Fig. 2, pp. 6–7) measures kernel $L^2$ error and intensity MSE only.
2. **No exact or near-exact reference.**
   - No HJB is solved and no optimum, closed form or constant quote is computed.
   - All six baselines are neural (p. 5).
   - "Regret" exists only as the theoretical object of Theorem 1 (p. 3) and is never evaluated
     numerically.
3. **No second-order law.**
   - Theorem 1 is a worst-case **upper** bound $\propto(\text{kernel }L^2\text{ error})^{1/2}$ with
     unspecified $C$ (pp. 3, 7). It is first-order-type (it also bounds the value error), not a
     quadratic law.
   - At the Fig. 2 parameters it is vacuous for $p\le8$ (this wiki's computation, item 1).
4. **A power-law truth is present, but weakly loaded.** The kernel is $b=1.15$ ↔ ε = 0.15. In
   Table 1, however, only 5.9 % of the kernel mass falls inside the episode (inferred, item 4).
   The reward ranking there is not a memory-regret measurement (reproduction flag, item 6).

**Gap status from this source: OPEN.** This verdict feeds [[memory-regret-gap-verification]]. What

- eq. (3) as the baseline lift (i), which proves weak for $t_0\ll T$;

- the "tie" regime as one documented instance of "memory irrelevant", explained only

- Fig. 2's frac015 setting ($b=1.15$, $t_0/T=0.01$) as the closest parameter match to the plan's
  grid.
