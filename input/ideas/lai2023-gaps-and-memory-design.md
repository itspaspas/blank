# Part A — Gap analysis of Lai, Gao & Li (2023), *Quant. Finance* 23(5): 777–797

Read: journal PDF `library/pdf/lai-2023-data-driven-deep.pdf` (journal pp. 777–797), against
[[lai-2023-data-driven-deep]]. Page numbers are journal pages.

**How it reads.** Option market orders on AAPL/BAC (CBOE 2019) are self-exciting with a power-law kernel
and a time-varying baseline, and only weakly cross-exciting between buy and sell. Direct policy
optimisation (Han–E, one FNN per side per decision time) trained on such flow beats policies trained on
Poisson flow out of sample, and a Hawkes-derived feature (ExpN, Intensity) adds a little more.
The paper's real weight sits on one inference: *the flow is non-Markov, so HJB/value-based methods are
out and a neural policy is the right tool* (pp. 778, 785).

## Major issues, ranked

1. **Kernel shape is never tested where it matters — in the control.** Every claim about non-Markov flow
   rests on goodness-of-fit (Table 1), never on a policy trained or evaluated under a Markov alternative.
   Implied L¹ norms from Table 2 (`α δ^{1−β}/(β−1)`, parameter averages — a caution, not a proof):
   0.28–1.86 for 15 of 16 rows and **7.67** for AAPL (1.01,1.02] 6–10 sell; **8 of 16 exceed 1**
   (one only at 1.0002). Caveat (referee): `δ^{1−β}` is extremely nonlinear, so norms from *averaged*
   parameters are not the average daily norm — a flag, not a proof of criticality. The norm is
   unit-invariant; the time unit (inferred ≈ minutes from μ₁ ≈ 25–33 vs 17–30 per 1-min bin in Fig. 2,
   not stated) matters for how much memory lies beyond one decision interval.
2. **The headline comparison confounds seasonality and clustering** (row 1); and the paper's own
   tables show the net adds little over a Constant quote (below).
3. **Statistics:** CI construction unreported, probable pseudo-replication (row 4); one training run, no seeds.
4. **Counterfactual fills, imposed κ, independent price/flow simulation (Remark 7), no adverse selection.**
   Added by the referee: eq. (14) treats N prints as independent depth draws, but clustered prints may be
   slices of one sweep, so clustering enters fills by assumption; the paper does not say whether
   same-timestamp prints are aggregated (tiny δ ≈ 0.0003 suggests not — check Lai 2021 thesis).
5. **No optimality benchmark** — and the learned policy is not shown near-optimal: **NoF ≤ Constant on
   BAC** (Table 8: −0.015, −0.002), although Constant lies inside NoF's function class; Poisson < Constant
   everywhere. The headline gap is mostly Constant−Poisson.
6. **Cross-contract flow assumed independent exactly where Γ and vega aggregate.**

## What is strong (protect it)

The empirical finding that option market orders cluster and have a strong intraday baseline is
convincing, the ExpN feature (Volterra forecast of the next-interval count) is a clean idea that avoids the
intensity-spike failure mode (p. 791), and the rolling train/test design with parameter uncertainty sampled
from daily fits is careful.

## If I changed three things in the paper

1. NHPP(μ(t)) comparator and day-clustered inference. 2. Train/test under exponential and lifted kernels.
3. Estimate κ from the trade file, and cross-contract excitation for Section 5.

# Part B — Candidate research design (most interesting gap: #1 above)

## Research Question

**Under power-law Hawkes order flow, is the regret of a market maker who tracks only *p* exponential
memory factors governed by the weighted forecast error between the true and the lifted flow — and
therefore how large is the decision-relevant memory (the smallest p at which the value of memory
V\*(p) plateaus) as a function of the kernel exponent β, the kernel mass beyond one decision interval, and the branching norm ‖g‖₁?**

*Operational form (referee + devil's advocate, 2026-10-29):*
- **V\*(p)** = optimum over policies that observe p filters of the history, estimated by direct
  optimisation under the *true* power-law flow with common random numbers; bracketed below by the NHPP
  policy (p = 0) and above by LIF. This replaces the unavailable "exact power-law optimum".
- **Misspecification regret** of the lift-HJB policy for p = 1, 2 run on the true flow is reported
  separately from V\*(p).
- **Regret law (the theorem-shaped part):** under the flow-driven quadratic closure, regret ≈ ½ h₂ ×
  ∫ (m^true_t(u) − m^{lift,p}_t(u))² du, where m_t(u) is the intensity forecast; this is computable from
  the kernel alone, so the simulation *validates* a prediction instead of just plotting a curve.
- **Analytic screen first:** with decision interval Δ, the kernel mass beyond one interval is
  (δ/(Δ+δ))^{β−1} (Δ = 1 in Table 2's inferred unit). From Table 2 this is 0.4–3% for β ≥ 1.45 and
  12–55% for β ≈ 1.07–1.2 (checked for five rows) — so the interesting regime is β ≲ 1.25 and the
  experiment should be centred there.
  *Erratum 2026-11-01 (Tier 0 recomputed all 16 rows; `output/data-analysis/memory-regret/results/screen.json` → `lai_table2.claim_a`): the β ≥ 1.45 rows span 0.37–18.3 % (7 of 8 outside 0.4–3 %; e.g. AAPL (1.01,1.02] 6–10 buy 11.9 %, BAC 3.0–18.3 %, because BAC's δ is 10–100× larger), and the β ≈ 1.07–1.2 rows span 26.7–55.9 %. The qualitative point (long memory beyond one interval only for small β) holds for AAPL, not for BAC.*

Falsifiable both ways: V\*(p) plateaus at p ≤ 2 within Monte-Carlo error by a pre-set threshold and
p_max (then the HJB/closed-form programme is not forfeited by non-Markov flow and Lai's rejection of
value-based methods was unnecessary — report as an equivalence result with a margin), or it keeps rising
up to p_max, or the regret law fails to match simulation. If a flexible baseline reproduces the "long
memory", the question dissolves into a finding about Lai's kernel selection, not about control.

## Re-evaluation after reading Barzykin 2026b and Acero–Plank–Zhang 2026 in full (2026-10-29)

Both read end to end (extracted text; Acero Thm 1 checked on the page image, p. 3).

**What they already cover**
- *Acero Thm 1 (p. 3):* regret of the greedy policy of a truncated mixture-of-exponentials kernel ≤
  C·(Σ_c [ε_l(K₁)+ε_u(K₂)+T^{3/2}(K₂−K₁)ϑ^c([K₁,K₂))/(√3 p)])^{1/2} — a **square-root** of the L² kernel
  error, C unspecified, and it requires the truncated kernel to be subcritical (‖ĝ‖₁ ≤ ρ\* < 1). So the
  claim "no rate exists" in my earlier draft is **wrong** and is dropped. Experiments: HSN with p = 2
  modes beats neural baselines by up to +32.5% on a fractional kernel (β = 1+α = 1.15), but **reward is
  never swept over p** (Fig. 2 sweeps kernel and intensity error only), and there is no exact optimum.
  Acero also reports that where the optimal spread depends only weakly on intensity (single exponential)
  memory-aware and memory-free networks **tie** — the null result is a real regime, not a hypothetical.
- *Barzykin §4–5:* a policy that never lifts: the Volterra–Riccati hierarchy driven by the conditional
  forecast curve m_t(u) (his eqs. 19–20, 23–32), validated against the exact lifted HJB for **one**
  exponential factor (Table 3: relative regret mean-VR 0.18/0.88/2.5%, noise-aware 0.08–2.2%,
  state-feedback 0.08/0.08/0.17%, Poisson 6.1/12.7/27.3%). His "power-law-like" memory (§6) is itself an
  **8-exponential mixture** (λ log-spaced on [2, 2500], w ∝ λ^{−0.65}); no exact benchmark, Poisson
  comparator only. He states that "a general error or convergence theory remains open" (p. 22) and writes
  the second-order functional expansion (eqs. 36–38) whose curvature kernel K_ij(t,q;u,v) is the object my
  regret law needs. His own fits (Table 1) are 3-exponential mixtures with ρ = 0.86–0.90 and he warns the
  branching mass may absorb an omitted common driver (pp. 2, 5, 22) — supporting the kernel-identifiability
  concern about Lai.

**What stays open** (verified against both texts)
1. A *predictive, second-order* regret law. Envelope argument: the policy error is first order in the
   forecast error Δm, the value loss second order, so regret ≈ ½ E∫∫ K Δm Δm (Barzykin's noise-aware
   correction ½∫∫ K Σ is the same functional with the covariance in place of ΔmΔm). Acero's bound is
   ½-power of the kernel error; whether the true exponent is 2 (in Δm) is untested.
2. Reward/regret **vs p on a power-law truth**, with the control-approximation error separated from the
   memory-truncation error (Barzykin's VR error is 0.1–2.5% and would otherwise be confounded with p).
3. Which **functional of the forecast curve** the quote depends on (the Riccati weights), hence why p = 2
   suffices in some regimes (Acero tie) and not in others.
4. The near-critical regime (‖g‖₁ ≳ 0.9), excluded by Acero's subcriticality condition and where Lai's
   fits and Barzykin's data sit.

**Design consequence:** the policy engine is Barzykin's Riccati layer fed with a *p-factor* forecast m̂ᵖ_t
versus the *exact* power-law forecast m_t, with an exact lifted-HJB anchor for truth = 2-exponential
mixture. The experiment is no longer "p-lift HJB vs deep policy"; the neural/direct-policy solver is a
cross-check, not the reference. The Method section below is superseded by the plan
(`lai2023-gaps-and-memory-plan.md`).

**Conviction, re-scored (judgment, not a computed quantity).**
- Original claim "a regret-vs-p curve on a power-law kernel is open": **≈ 35%** that this alone is a
  contribution a referee will not call incremental to Acero (theorem + p = 2 experiments) and Barzykin
  (hierarchy + exact exponential anchor).
- Reframed claim "second-order regret law + decision-relevant functional + regime map, validated against an
  exact anchor": **≈ 55–60%** — the open points above are explicit in both papers (Barzykin p. 22: error
  theory open; Acero: reward-vs-p absent), and the law is cheap to test (Δm needs only the kernel).
- What would move it down: the law fails the exact anchor (second order not attained because the lift's
  policy also errs through the *information* it lacks, not only through Δm); regret at p = 2 below solver
  error everywhere (then a short equivalence note only); or Jusselin/Martin–Tse full texts already
  contain a regret-vs-p table (not read in full yet).

## Why this is the contribution

- Theory says lifts converge with **no rate** or only in kernel L¹/L² error (Jusselin, Martin–Tse, Acero
  et al., Khabou — [[markov-lift]]); nobody turns that into a **regret curve on an empirically calibrated
  power-law kernel**. Kernel-norm error is not decision error.
- It decides a methodological fork this project sits on: closed form/exact HJB (needs Markov) vs learned
  policy (Lai). It is the empirical counterpart to Stage 2a's frozen-forecast closure.
- It reuses published parameters (Lai Table 2), so a first version needs **no data purchase**.

## Method (sketch; pre-registration in the plan)

0. **Control for seasonality first (replicates Part A row 1).** Flow models compared: Poisson, NHPP(μ(t)),
   exponential Hawkes, p-exponential Hawkes, power-law Hawkes — same μ(t), matched total intensity.
1. **Truth:** power-law Hawkes swept over (β, ‖g‖₁) with ‖g‖₁ < 1 *by construction* (β ∈ {1.1, 1.25, 1.4,
   1.8}, ‖g‖₁ ∈ {0.5, 0.8, 0.95}) — not Lai's averaged Table 2, whose norms are unreliable. Lai's rows
   are used only as realistic anchor points. Time unit fixed by checking Lai 2021 (thesis).
2. **Control problem to specify before any code:** the project's flow-driven GLFT/BEGV-type problem
   (one asset first) or Lai's discrete-time option problem with Bernoulli fills? Lai's is **dynamic
   programming**, not HJB; a grid DP with p ≤ 2 factors still has 2p + (q, t) ≈ 6 dimensions. Default
   recommendation: the project's GLFT-type problem, where the Stage 2a closure supplies the regret law.
3. **Policies:** (a) NHPP-optimal (p = 0); (b) lift-optimal for p = 1, 2 (grid) and p = 3, 4 (neural
   solver, solver error reported separately); (c) direct-optimisation V\*(p) with p filters, ≥ 5 seeds,
   Constant quote included in the function class and checked to be beaten (Lai's NoF is not on BAC);
   (d) LIF as a ceiling only. **One arm lets the maker's fills excite the flow** (fill-driven).
4. **Evaluation:** Monte-Carlo under the *true* power-law flow, common random numbers, bootstrap clustered
   by simulated day/path block, convergence of the reference in p shown, equivalence margin and p_max
   frozen in the plan.
5. **Real-data leg (optional, needs data):** re-fit exponential, p-exponential, power-law with
   information criteria and a flexible/Cox baseline on option or futures trades; test whether power-law
   survives. Data candidates: CBOE (paid), Binance/Tardis (free tier).
6. **Cross-contract extension (second paper):** estimate the cross-contract excitation block on an
   option chain and quantify how independence understates aggregate Γ/vega violation rates (Part A #6).

## Risks

- **Circular reference / trivial null (devil's advocate):** no exact optimum under a power-law truth;
  differences between a large-p lift and a deep policy may sit inside solver noise, and regret at p = 2
  may simply be tiny (Lai's flow is exogenous, memory enters only through a forecast). Mitigation: V\*(p)
  with an equivalence margin, the analytic regret law, the β ≲ 1.25 focus, and the fill-driven arm.
- **Truth may be an artefact:** Lai's power-law rests on KS fits; Filimonov–Sornette 2015 show spurious
  near-criticality from non-stationary baselines. The synthetic design is immune; the real-data leg is not.
- **Prior art (Scout, 2026-10-29, moderate depth):** no paper found that measures policy/P&L regret of a
  sum-of-exponentials lift under a power-law Hawkes truth. Closest: Jusselin 2021 (gains for p = 0, 1, 2 on
  exponential-type flow; from an abstract summary), Barzykin 2026b, Khabou 2025, Acero 2026, and
  Jain et al. 2025 (arXiv 2510.26438, remark that a sum-of-exponentials fix did not help; not in the wiki).
  **Barzykin 2026b and Acero 2026 must be read in full for a p-sweep before any novelty claim.**
- Referee doubts the "no rate" claim: (t+δ)^{−β} is completely monotone, so quadrature lifts with rates
  probably exist — verify in Acero Prop. 1 / Khabou before asserting a gap.
- Journal fit: Quant. Finance plausible; SIAM J. Financial Math needs the regret law, not only the curve.
